# Keep the map's interpretation current

Recheck the map's claims and scope as its sources change.
A link can still resolve after its target stops supporting the explanation.

**Read the evidence again**

Follow enough linked material to check the important claims.
Compare membership with the stated scope and look for relevant omissions.
A changed claim or stronger counterexample can invalidate a grouping.

Structural renames can preserve indexed links.
Arbitrary edits and unmounted sources can still leave stale addresses.

**Choose what time the map describes**

Update a living overview in place.
Give a dated assessment an explicit effective date and its evidence.
Git records when bytes changed, which may differ from the time a claim describes.
A recent edit does not establish review of the whole synthesis.

**Revise the interpretation or its limits**

Update affected prose and connections when the interpretation changes.
Keep historical reasoning separately when the task needs it addressable.
See [[supersede]].

For a computed index, check its derivation and input scope instead of editing the output.
State material gaps so readers can distinguish established relationships from questions.

Read the strongest conclusion beside the source most likely to challenge it.
Revise what the current material no longer supports:

- The conclusion
- Its evidence
- The stated scope
