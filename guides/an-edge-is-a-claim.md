# Make the relationship legible

Link when the referent's identity and its relationship to the source help the task.
A shared word is insufficient: two notes can use the same name for different things.

**State the supported relationship**

Treat these as distinct relationships:

- Passing mention
- Dependency
- Contradiction
- Supporting evidence
- Any other relationship the evidence supports
Choose the strength the evidence supports.
The engine can resolve targets and check typed endpoints, but cannot establish that the relationship is true.

Link artifacts when identity matters to the task:

- Navigation or attribution
- Explanation or a consumer's operation
- Another use that needs this referent

Repeated or weakly motivated links can hide the useful ones.

**Use the existing inverse**

For “what points here,” use the engine's backlinks and filter by the intended relation when needed.
Avoid maintaining a second list that only duplicates the same authored fact.

Two directions can carry independent meanings.
A human-facing overview can also deliberately present a derived inverse.

**Explain why the links belong**

A map's links should show why the gathered notes belong together.
A typed relation should express the distinction its consumer needs.
See [[author-a-map]] and [[modeling-composition]].

Read the sentence around each new link and name the relationship it asserts.
Open enough of the target to check the referent and support that relationship.
